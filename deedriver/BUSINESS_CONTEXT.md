# DeedFlow — Business Context & Platform Mechanics

## What DeedFlow Is

DeedFlow is a homeowner-controlled equity tokenization platform.
It converts residential real estate equity into digital tokens that the
homeowner owns and controls — without transferring the deed, without
taking on debt, and without surrendering ownership to any institution.

The platform is explicitly anti-corporate home ownership. DeedFlow
is architectural designed so the platform itself can never acquire
residential homes. All tokenized assets remain owner-occupied and
owner-controlled throughout their participation on the platform.

---

## The Core Problem DeedFlow Solves

American homeowners hold approximately $32 trillion in home equity.
This capital is almost entirely illiquid — it generates no return,
cannot be deployed without taking on debt, and can only be accessed
through mechanisms that transfer significant value to intermediaries.

## How the Mechanics Work

### Step 1 — Tokenization
- DeedFlow mints 1,000 DEED tokens representing 100% of the homeowner's net equity
- Net equity = property value minus any existing mortgage balance
- The platform works with mortgaged homes — tokens represent the equity layer only
- The mortgage holder's first lien is untouched and senior in all scenarios
- The homeowner receives all 1,000 tokens immediately
- The deed never transfers. Title stays with the homeowner at all times.

### Step 2 — Pledge (not borrowing)
- The homeowner pledges a portion of tokens (typically 60–80%) to DeedFlow Capital
- DeedFlow Capital advances capital against the pledged tokens at ~70% LTV
- The homeowner never incurs personal debt — the advance is against token collateral
- The homeowner retains unpledged tokens fully unencumbered
- The pledge can be reduced or exited at any time

### Step 3 — Fund Deployment
- Advanced capital is deployed into the DeedFlow Capital fund strategy
- Fund managed by institutional-grade team with verified 5-year track record
- Historical returns: 25%+ annually at very low risk over 5 consecutive years
- HomeToken product target: 12% gross return (conservative vs. track record)
- Fund is a closed-loop structure: advances capital, deploys it, returns flow back

### Step 4 — Yield Distribution
- Returns distributed quarterly to homeowner's DeedFlow account
- Net of platform fees (see fee structure below)
- Homeowner can withdraw, reinvest, or accumulate
- Exit: reduce or remove pledge at any time to recover full token position

## Three Use Cases

### Use Case 1 — Yield Generation (HELOC Replacement)
The primary product. Homeowner tokenizes equity, pledges tokens, earns
fund returns quarterly. No debt. No deed transfer. No broker.

**Best for:** Equity-rich homeowners (45–70) seeking returns on trapped capital
without the burden of debt service. Works especially well for high-value
properties in markets with significant appreciation.

### Use Case 2 — Reverse Mortgage Replacement
Instead of pledging for yield, homeowner sells tokens on a controlled,
self-determined annual schedule.

- Homeowner retains all 1,000 tokens at origination
- Each year, elects to sell a defined quantity (e.g., 50 tokens = 5% of equity)
- Proceeds paid immediately — no interest accrual, no compounding liability
- Homeowner continues living in property and retaining remaining tokens
- Estate retains all unsold tokens upon death

**Why it's better than a reverse mortgage:**

| Feature | Reverse Mortgage | DeedFlow |
|---------|-----------------|----------|
| Interest accrues | Yes — compounds | No |
| Mandatory insurance | Yes (MIP) | No |
| Homeowner controls pace | No | Yes |
| Estate retains residual equity | Often little/none | All unsold tokens |
| Upfront fees | $6,000+ | Sub-1% |

**Best for:** Seniors 62+ seeking controlled liquidity without lender predation.

### Use Case 3 — P2P Marketplace (Broker Replacement)
Tokenized homes trade on the DeedFlow marketplace at dramatically
lower transaction costs than traditional brokerage.

- Fractional token sales to accredited investors
- Whole-property sale via token transfer at 1–1.5% vs. 5–6% brokerage
- Estate settlement: heirs sell tokens immediately without traditional listing

**Fee comparison on a $1.5M property:**
- Traditional brokerage (5.5%): $82,500
- DeedFlow marketplace (1.5%): $22,500
- **Net savings: $60,000**

- Designed so the platform can never acquire residential homes

## Risk Factors

### Property Value Decline
Token values pegged to AVM. Significant price decline may trigger LTV
adjustment or margin call. Platform maintains 70% LTV conservative threshold.
Annual AVM refresh with dispute mechanism. Build in margin call language
into pledge agreement.

### Fund Performance Risk
Returns are investment-linked, not guaranteed. The 12% target is
conservative relative to the fund's track record, but past performance
does not guarantee future results. Disclose this prominently everywhere.

### Lien Priority Risk
Existing first mortgage holder has senior claim. Token holders hold
subordinate equity interests. If homeowner defaults on mortgage, first
lien holder's claim takes precedence. Clearly disclosed in all materials.

### Liquidity Risk (Marketplace)
Early-phase marketplace may have limited depth. Set expectations clearly.
The yield product does not depend on marketplace liquidity.

### Due-on-Sale Clause
Most mortgages contain due-on-sale clauses triggered by property interest
transfers. Legal analysis indicates synthetic token layer (not a recorded
interest) likely does not trigger this — but lender consent should be
sought where possible. Do not state this is risk-free in copy.

## Regulatory & Compliance Context

- Wyoming-incorporated entity
- Launch structure: Regulation D Rule 506(c) — accredited investors only
- No SEC registration required at launch
- Accredited investor = net worth >$1M excluding primary residence OR
  income >$200K individual / $300K joint for two consecutive years
- Tokens are almost certainly securities under the Howey test (Use Case 1)
- Investment Adviser registration required for fund management component
- Proactive CFPB engagement recommended before marketing reverse mortgage alternative
- Wyoming is the most favorable U.S. tokenization jurisdiction — explicit statutory
  recognition of digital asset property rights, SPDI charter available
